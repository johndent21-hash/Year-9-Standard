Year 9 5.2 Yearly Exam: Help Sheet

Key ideas, formulas and worked examples for every skill in the practice exams

How to use it: keep this sheet beside you while you do a practice exam. When you mark your work, the answers page tells you which topic to read (for example D4 or M6) for any question you got wrong. Read that card, then try the question again. Remember: in the real exam each question gives you the formula you need as a "Note".

Section 1: Data

D1Types of data

  • Categorical: words or groups, e.g. hair colour, favourite sport, shirt size.
  • Numerical discrete: counted, so whole numbers, e.g. number of pets, goals, people.
  • Numerical continuous: measured, so it can be any value, e.g. height, mass, time, temperature, volume.

Tip: ask "do I count it or measure it?"

D2Shape of a distribution

  • Symmetrical: both sides look the same; most scores in the middle.
  • Positively skewed: scores bunched at the low end, tail stretching to the right.
  • Negatively skewed: scores bunched at the high end, tail stretching to the left.
  • Cluster: where most of the scores are grouped.

Tip: the skew is named after the side the tail is on.

D3Reading graphs

  • Dot plot and histogram: each column's height is the frequency (how many). The mode is the score under the tallest column, not the height of that column.
  • Total: add up the heights of all the columns.
  • Percentage = parttotal × 100%
Example: columns 5, 9, 4, 2. Total = 20. The column of 5 as a percentage: 5 ÷ 20 × 100% = 25%.

D4Mean, median, mode and range

Mean = sum of the scores ÷ number of scores
Range = highest score − lowest score
  • Median: the middle score when the scores are in order. With an even number of scores, average the two middle ones.
  • Mode: the score that occurs most often.
Example: 4, 7, 7, 9, 13. Mean = 40 ÷ 5 = 8; median = 7 (3rd score); mode = 7; range = 13 − 4 = 9.

D5Outliers

An outlier is a score far away from all the others.

  • It changes the mean the most (the mean uses every score).
  • The median and mode hardly change, so the median describes data with an outlier better.
Example: 6, 7, 7, 8, 32. Mean = 12 but median = 7. Without 32: mean 7, median 7.

D6Stem-and-leaf plots: comparing two data sets

  • Read each score with the key: 4 | 6 = 46.
  • In a back-to-back plot the left side is read backwards: a left leaf of 3 on stem 5 is 53.
  • Which group scores higher? Compare the medians (or means).
  • Which is more consistent? The one with the smaller range: its scores are closer together.

Always give the numbers in your reason: "Team A, because its range is 12, smaller than Team B's 30."

Section 2: Surface area and volume

M1Units of capacity and volume

1 kL = 1000 L    1 L = 1000 mL
1 cm³ = 1 mL    1 L = 1000 cm³    1 m³ = 1000 L
  • Big unit → small unit: × 1000. Small unit → big unit: ÷ 1000.
Examples: 3.2 kL = 3200 L; 850 mL = 0.85 L; 24 000 cm³ = 24 L.

M2Area of shapes

ShapeArea
RectangleA = lb
TriangleA = ½bh
ParallelogramA = bh
Kite or rhombusA = ½xy (x, y = diagonals)

Use the perpendicular height (the dashed line at right angles), not the sloping side.

Example: triangle, base 10 cm, height 7 cm: ½ × 10 × 7 = 35 cm².

M3Circles

Circumference: C = 2πr    Area: A = πr²
  • The radius r is half the diameter. If the line goes right across the circle, halve it first.
  • Use the π key and round only at the end.
Example: diameter 12 cm, so r = 6. C = 2 × π × 6 = 37.7 cm; A = π × 6² = 113.1 cm².

M4Composite shapes (L-shapes)

  • Perimeter: add every outside side. Find missing sides first (opposite sides add up).
  • Area: split into two rectangles and add, or take a big rectangle minus the missing corner.
  • Cost = area × price per m².

M5Volume

Rectangular prism: V = lwh    Cylinder: V = πr²h
Any prism: V = area of the end × length
Example: box 40 cm × 20 cm × 15 cm: V = 12 000 cm³ = 12 L.
Cylinder r = 3 cm, h = 10 cm: V = π × 3² × 10 = 282.7 cm³.

M6Surface area (closed solids)

  • Box: 6 faces in 3 matching pairs.
    SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh
  • Cylinder: 2 circles + the curved rectangle.
    SA = 2πr² + 2πrh
Example: box 10 × 4 × 3 cm: 2(40) + 2(30) + 2(12) = 80 + 60 + 24 = 164 cm².

Don't forget to double each face: there are 6 faces, not 3.

Section 3: Mixed Topics

A1Substitution

Replace each letter with its number, then use BODMAS. Powers come before ×.

Example: if a = 3 and b = 4, then 2a² + b = 2 × 3² + 4 = 2 × 9 + 4 = 22 (not 6² + 4).

A2Simplifying

  • Only like terms (same letters) can be added or subtracted: 5a + 2b − 3a + b = 2a + 3b.
  • Multiply: numbers × numbers, letters × letters: 3a × 4b = 12ab.
  • Divide: cancel numbers and letters: 20xy ÷ 4x = 5y.

A3Expanding brackets

Multiply every term inside by the term outside. A negative outside changes every sign inside.

Example: 3(2x + 1) − 2(x − 4)
= 6x + 3 − 2x + 8 = 4x + 11

−2 × −4 = +8.

A4Factorising

Take out the highest common factor (HCF) and put the rest in brackets. Check by expanding.

Examples: 8a + 12 = 4(2a + 3)
m² − 6m = m(m − 6)

A5Index laws

am × an = am+n    am ÷ an = am−n    (am)n = amn
Example: 3y⁴ × 5y² = 15y⁶ (multiply the numbers, add the indices).

E1Solving equations

  1. Expand any brackets.
  2. Collect the pronumerals on one side (take away the smaller one).
  3. Undo + and −, then × and ÷. Do the same to both sides.
Example: 5x − 2(x + 3) = x + 8
5x − 2x − 6 = x + 8 → 3x − 6 = x + 8
2x = 14 → x = 7. Check: 35 − 20 = 15 ✓

E2Formulas and word problems

In C = 40 + 25h, 40 is the fixed fee and 25 is the cost per hour.

  • Find C: substitute. For h = 3: C = 40 + 75 = $115.
  • Given C: write an equation and solve. 40 + 25h = 190 → 25h = 150 → h = 6.

F1Percentages, discount and profit

Discount = % × marked price;   sale price = marked price − discount
Profit = selling price − cost price
% profit = profitcost price × 100%
Example: $80 less 15%: discount $12, sale price $68.
Bought $200, sold $260: profit $60; 60 ÷ 200 × 100% = 30%.

F2Simple interest

I = Prn   (P = amount invested, r = rate as a decimal, n = years)
Example: $3000 at 5% p.a. for 4 years: I = 3000 × 0.05 × 4 = $600.

Change the % to a decimal: 3.5% = 0.035.

F3Earning money

1 year = 52 weeks = 26 fortnights = 12 months
  • Salary: weekly = ÷ 52, fortnightly = ÷ 26, monthly = ÷ 12.
  • Overtime: time-and-a-half = rate × 1.5 (double time = rate × 2). The first 38 hours are at the normal rate.
  • Commission = % × sales. With a retainer, add the fixed amount.
Example: $30/h, 41 hours: 38 × 30 = $1140; 3 × 30 × 1.5 = $135; total $1275.

T1Pythagoras’ theorem

c² = a² + b²   (c = hypotenuse, the longest side, opposite the right angle)
  • Hypotenuse: add the squares, then √.
  • Shorter side (e.g. a ladder's height up a wall): subtract the squares, then √.
Example: ladder 5 m, foot 1.5 m from the wall: h² = 5² − 1.5² = 22.75, so h = 4.77 m.

T2Trigonometry: finding an angle

sin θ = opphyp   cos θ = adjhyp   tan θ = oppadj   (SOH CAH TOA)
  1. Label the sides from the angle: opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse.
  2. Pick the ratio that uses the two sides you know.
  3. Use the inverse key: SHIFT cos.
Example: adj 1.5 m, hyp 5 m: cos θ = 1.5 ÷ 5 → θ = cos⁻¹(0.3) = 73°.

Check the calculator shows D (degrees).
