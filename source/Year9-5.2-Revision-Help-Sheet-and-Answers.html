1 Financial mathematics

Ways of being paid

  • Wage: paid per hour. Pay = hourly rate × hours.
  • Salary: a fixed amount per year, paid in equal parts.
  • Commission: a percentage of sales. Sometimes paid on top of a retainer (a fixed weekly amount).
  • Overtime: time-and-a-half = rate × 1.5; double time = rate × 2.
Time conversions for pay
1 year = 52 weeks = 26 fortnights = 12 months
Weekly pay = salary ÷ 52
Fortnightly pay = salary ÷ 26
Monthly pay = salary ÷ 12
Example: $25/h for 38 h, then 4 h at time-and-a-half.
Normal: 25 × 38 = $950. Overtime: 4 × 25 × 1.5 = $150. Total = $1100.

Tax and deductions

  • Gross pay = total earned before anything is taken out.
  • Net pay = gross pay − deductions (tax, super, union fees …).
  • Taxable income = gross income − allowable tax deductions (work-related costs).

Buying and selling

  • Profit = selling price − cost price
  • % profit = profitcost price × 100%
  • Discount = marked price × rate
  • Sale price = marked price − discount
    (or marked price × (100% − rate))
  • GST is 10%: price with GST = price × 1.1
Simple interest:   I = Prn    P = principal (amount invested), r = interest rate per year as a decimal, n = number of years.
Months → years: divide by 12 (e.g. 30 months = 2.5 years).   Total value = P + I.
Example: $4000 at 3% p.a. for 18 months.   I = 4000 × 0.03 × 1.5 = $180.
Watch out: change the % into a decimal (3.5% = 0.035) and the time into years before you use I = Prn.

3 Algebraic techniques and indices

Simplifying

  • Like terms have the same pronumerals with the same powers: 3x and −5x are like terms; 3x and 3x² are not.
  • Only add or subtract like terms: 7x + 2y − 3x = 4x + 2y.
  • Multiply: numbers × numbers, letters × letters: 6m × 4n = 24mn.
  • Divide: write as a fraction and cancel: 20ab ÷ 5a = 4b.
  • Substitution: replace each letter with its value in brackets, then use BODMAS. If q = 3, 3q² = 3 × 3² = 27 (square first).

Expanding (removing brackets)

  • Multiply every term inside by the term outside: 4(3x − 2) = 12x − 8.
  • A negative outside changes every sign: −3(4k − 7) = −12k + 21.
  • Expand each bracket, then collect like terms.

Factorising (putting brackets back)

  • Find the highest common factor (HCF) of the numbers and the letters, and put it outside.
  • 12y² − 18y = 6y(2y − 3).
  • Check by expanding your answer.
Index laws
am × an = am+n
am ÷ an = am−n
(am)n = amn
a0 = 1
a−n = 1an
(ab)n = anbn
Example: 2a³b × 6ab² = (2 × 6) × a3+1 × b1+2 = 12a⁴b³.   12y⁹ ÷ 4y³ = (12 ÷ 4)y9−3 = 3y⁶.
Watch out: multiply the coefficients (the numbers) but add the indices. A letter on its own has index 1 (a = a¹).

4 Pythagoras’ theorem and trigonometry

Pythagoras’ theorem (right-angled triangles only)
c² = a² + b², where c is the hypotenuse: the longest side, opposite the right angle.
Finding the hypotenuse: c = √(a² + b²)  (add the squares)
Finding a shorter side: a = √(c² − b²)  (subtract the squares)
Example: Hypotenuse 13, one side 5. Other side = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12.

Trigonometry: label the sides from the angle θ you are using.

SOH CAH TOA
sin θ = oppositehypotenuse    cos θ = adjacenthypotenuse    tan θ = oppositeadjacent

Finding a side

  1. Label opp, adj and hyp from the given angle.
  2. Choose the ratio that uses the side you know and the side you want.
  3. If the unknown is on top: multiply.  x = 20 × sin 35°
  4. If the unknown is on the bottom: divide.  x = 20 ÷ cos 35°

Finding an angle

  • Work out the ratio, then use the inverse key (shift):
    cos θ = 1.34.8 → θ = cos⁻¹(1.3 ÷ 4.8) = 74°
  • Degrees and minutes: 1° = 60′. Use the ° ′ ″ key. 32.5° = 32°30′.
  • Check your calculator is in degrees mode (D on the screen).
Angles of elevation and depression are measured from the horizontal: up to an object (elevation) or down to an object (depression).

5 Data analysis

Types of data

  • Categorical: words or categories (colour, car type, S/M/L, poor/good/excellent).
  • Numerical discrete: counted, so only whole values (number of pets, goals).
  • Numerical continuous: measured, so any value in a range (mass, time, length, temperature).

Measures of centre and spread

  • Mean = sum of scoresnumber of scores
  • Median = middle score when the scores are in order. With an even number of scores, average the two middle ones. Its position is the n + 12th score.
  • Mode = the most common score.
  • Range = highest − lowest.

Shape of a distribution

  • Symmetrical: both sides look roughly the same.
  • Positively skewed: scores bunch on the left (low end) with a long tail to the right.
  • Negatively skewed: scores bunch on the right (high end) with a tail to the left.
  • Cluster: where most scores are grouped. Outlier: a score far from the rest.

Outliers change the mean a lot but the median very little, so the median describes data with an outlier better.

Comparing two sets (back-to-back stem-and-leaf plots): the higher median (or mean) means "better" or "more"; the smaller range means more consistent.

Reading graphs: read stem-and-leaf plots with the key (3 | 5 = 35). In a back-to-back plot, the left-hand leaves are read from the stem outward. Histogram: total = add the column heights.

Example: 3, 5, 6, 6, 8, 20.   Mean = 48 ÷ 6 = 8.   Median = (6 + 6) ÷ 2 = 6.   Mode = 6.   Range = 20 − 3 = 17. The outlier 20 pulls the mean up, so the median is the better measure.
Percentage of a total: parttotal × 100%. Check that your categories add up to the total.

6 Equations

  • Whatever you do to one side, do the same to the other side.
  • Undo operations in reverse order using inverse operations: + and − undo each other; × and ÷ undo each other.
  • Brackets: expand first.
  • Pronumerals on both sides: subtract the smaller pronumeral term from both sides first.
  • Fractions: multiply both sides by the denominator.
  • Check: substitute your answer back into the original equation.
  • Word problems: pick a letter for the unknown, write the equation, solve it, and answer in words with units.
Example 1: 5x − 4 = 2x + 11
−2x:   3x − 4 = 11
+4:   3x = 15
÷3:   x = 5   Check: 21 = 21 ✓
Example 2: 2(a + 3) = 18
Expand: 2a + 6 = 18 → 2a = 12 → a = 6
Example 3: x3 + 4 = 9 → x3 = 5 → x = 15

7 Linear relationships

Key formulas
Gradient m = riserun = y₂ − y₁x₂ − x₁
Gradient–intercept form: y = mx + c
  m = gradient, c = y-intercept
Midpoint = (x₁ + x₂2, y₁ + y₂2)
Distance = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)
  • Positive gradient: line goes up from left to right. Negative gradient: line goes down.
  • Horizontal line y = k: gradient 0. Vertical line x = k: gradient undefined.
  • Parallel lines have the same gradient.
  • Graphing: make a table of values (e.g. x = −1, 0, 1, 2), plot the points and rule a line through them.
  • x-intercept: set y = 0 and solve. y-intercept: set x = 0.
  • Does a point lie on the line? Substitute x and check you get the same y.
  • Real-life lines: in C = 90 + 65h, the 90 is a fixed starting cost (the y-intercept) and 65 is the rate per hour (the gradient).
Example: Line through (1, 3) and (4, 9).
m = 9 − 34 − 1 = 2. Substitute (1, 3): 3 = 2(1) + c, so c = 1.
Equation: y = 2x + 1.   Midpoint = (2.5, 6).
